Math Fasters: game plan

A browser arcade game for practising math facts on a tablet, phone or computer. First playable scene: Asteroid Blast, multiplication with numbers up through 7. Target: kids testing within 4 hours.

This is an original game with the same kind of play you remember: quick fact drills wrapped in arcade scenes, with levels to climb. It has its own name, characters, art and sounds and does not copy Math Blaster's.

2. Asteroid Blast: how it plays

  • One fact at a time. Four asteroids drift down, each carrying a number. Tap the right one and the ship blasts it. Big touch targets; mouse and number keys 1 to 4 also work.
  • Three shields. A wrong tap or a right answer that reaches the red line costs a shield. Zero shields is game over.
  • Waves of 10 facts. Each wave falls a little faster. Clearing a wave restores one shield. A mission is 3 waves.
  • Score rewards speed. Faster blasts score more, and a streak of right answers builds a multiplier.
  • Misses still teach. On a miss the game pauses a beat and shows the full fact (6 × 7 = 42). That fact comes back two or three turns later, and more often in later games.
  • Wrong answers are believable. Decoys come from neighbouring facts (6 × 6, 7 × 7, 6 × 8) so she can't win by ruling out silly numbers.

3. Levelling up

Each kid follows a mission map. A mission is a set of facts plus a speed. Finishing with at least 80% right unlocks the next one. Each mission awards 1 to 3 stars, and stars raise her rank (Cadet, Pilot, Captain, Commander).

MissionFactsSpeed
1 Launch pad×1, ×2Slow
2 Moon run×3, with ×1 and ×2 mixed inSlow
3 Red planet×4Medium
4 Asteroid belt×5Medium

Under the map, the game tracks every single fact: how often she gets it right and how fast. Weak facts show up more often. A grown-up can see the grid at a glance.

What a grown-up can adjust per kid

  • Which tables are in play (default 1 through 7; up to 12 available).
  • Speed: slow, medium or fast.
  • Skip the mission map and practise a chosen set of tables freely.
  • Sound on or off. Reset progress.

Then a fly-through-the-gate cave scene, and a "one change" logic puzzle: build a chain of creatures where each neighbour differs in exactly one feature (shape, eyes, arms or colour), with our own creatures and art.

Each stage ends with something playable at the same link. I'll track the work as GitHub issues and stop for your feedback after Stage 2.

5. How I'll build it

  • Plain web page. HTML, CSS and JavaScript with a canvas for the action. No framework and no build step, so it loads fast, is simple to host, and is easy to change later.
  • Art and sound made in code. Ships, rocks and explosions are drawn shapes; sounds are synthesised beeps and booms. No downloaded assets, so nothing to license.
  • Progress saved on the device. Profiles live in the browser's storage on each tablet or phone. Nothing is sent anywhere, and no kid names go in the repo. Trade-off: progress does not follow a kid from one device to another.
  • Tested math. Fact picking, decoys, scoring and unlock rules sit in a separate module with automated tests. I'll also play it in a real browser at phone and tablet sizes before handing it over.
  • Add to home screen. It will open full-screen from an icon like an app.
